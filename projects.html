AI training & development

I work hands-on with the current AI development tools and train others to use them well. The sessions are built around real work rather than demonstrations — students and faculty leave with something they can apply the same week.

Tools I work with

Claude

Long-context reasoning across datasheets, standards and codebases. Used for design review, verification strategy and HDL drafting.

Cursor

AI-native editor for refactoring, understanding unfamiliar legacy code and scaffolding test suites inside a real workflow.

Gemini AI

Multimodal analysis and large-context research support, including document, diagram and dataset reasoning.

ChatGPT

Everyday assistant for explanation, rapid prototyping, structured learning material and iterative problem solving.

Applied development

AI-assisted engineering

Applying these tools to real delivery work: understanding large and unfamiliar codebases, structured refactoring, automated test generation and documentation.

Curriculum integration

Introducing AI tooling into VLSI and software courses as a supervised exercise — students use it, then must prove and verify the result.

Critical evaluation

Teaching the failure modes: hallucinated specifications, plausible but unverified code, and the discipline needed to catch both before they reach a design or a student.

My position on this: these tools are fast junior engineers, not authorities. The gain comes from asking precisely and being able to verify the answer — which is exactly the habit a VLSI or software course should be building anyway.
